### Drill settings — one panel, one hook

Vocab Drill, Anime Vocab and Vocab SRS render **the same settings drawer**: `src/components/DrillSettingsPanel.jsx`, fed by `useDrillSettings(prefix)` from `src/hooks/useDrillSettings.js`. They previously kept three hand-maintained copies of a checkbox list, and the labels had already drifted ("Show furigana" vs "Show furigana on front"). Don't add a fourth — a new drill calls the same hook and renders the same panel.

**Grouped by the part of the card each setting changes**, which is the question being asked when the drawer opens:

| Group | Rows |
|---|---|
| Card front | Furigana, Audio |
| Card back | Reading (Below / Above), Meaning, Audio |
| Details | Show under card, Sentence, Kanji (the panel's light / dark switch lives on the panel itself) |
| Sentence (only while the sentence shows) | Translation (Off / Blurred / On), Furigana (Off / New / All) |
| Audio | Voice (Male / Female), Play sentence, Backup voice |
| Interface | Sound effects, Visual effects, Pixel font, Streak counter |

Audio is **not** a group of its own: playing the word is one of the things a face does, so `frontAudio` (plays as the card arrives) and `backAudio` (plays on flip) are rows in the two faces, and the audio group keeps only the global decision of which voice speaks. That replaced a master `audio-enabled` switch plus SRS's `autoplay-audio` / `-front` / `-back` trio.

**Voice vs backup voice.** `voice` (`'male'` | `'female'`) picks between the two recorded Voicevox voices — `audioSourceForVoice()` maps it onto the `voicevox-11` / `voicevox-2` speaker strings the rest of the audio code still speaks. `backupVoice` is a browser speech-synthesis voice name, and it reads **any word with no recording** — the drills now fall through to it unconditionally, where they used to only speak when the user had explicitly picked the retired "Browser TTS" source. Consequences: there is no longer a way to force browser TTS over an existing recording, and a card that used to be silent now gets spoken.

Since clips are keyed by what is spoken rather than recorded per word, neither drill can tell in advance whether one exists, so **the fallback fires on playback failure, not on a missing URL**. Both drills go through the shared `useVoicevoxPlayer` hook (`src/hooks/useVoicevoxPlayer.js`) — Web Audio API (`AudioContext` + `AudioBufferSourceNode`), not `HTMLMediaElement.play()`, because the latter re-checks the browser's autoplay/user-activation policy on *every* call, and a gamepad button press never grants user activation per spec, so `play()` intermittently rejected and silently fell back to TTS even when the clip existed — this hit mobile browsers hardest, since `setTimeout`-deferred autoplay (the front-of-card audio) also loses the activation chain from the tap that triggered it. An `AudioContext` only needs activation once, at `resume()` time, and then stays usable for any later scheduled playback regardless of trigger. `play()` resolves false when a clip 404s or decode fails, which is what `speakCard()` (`VocabSrsDrill.jsx`) / `playWordAudio()` (`VocabPage.jsx`) check to fall back to the backup voice. `play(url, { onEnded })` is how SRS chains sentence audio after the word finishes — `onEnded` is guarded by the same token-based supersede check `stop()`/a newer `play()` already use, so an aborted/replaced clip doesn't fire a stale chain. **`VocabSrsDrill` used to have its own separate `HTMLAudioElement`-based path** (`started()`, raw `new Audio()`) — that was the actual cause of a long-standing bug where SRS review, especially on mobile and with a Bluetooth controller, would intermittently play the backup voice instead of an existing Voicevox clip (and could still play the wrong/stale audio after Undo, since pausing an in-flight `<audio>` before its `playing` event fired aborted its `play()` promise and looked exactly like a failed clip). Migrated to `useVoicevoxPlayer` to fix it — a new audio call site should go through this hook rather than calling `HTMLMediaElement.play()` directly and ignoring the result.

**Rows that don't apply are not rendered** — never disabled-with-an-explanation. Two deliberate exceptions, because each is the same row a moment later: the last of Sentence / Kanji still on is disabled (the details panel always shows at least one), and Play sentence is disabled while no sentence is showing. `hasRecordedVoices={false}` drops the Voice row (Anime Vocab pulls its words from subtitles, so no recordings exist for any of them); an empty `backupVoices` drops the Backup voice row (a device with no speech voices has nothing to choose between). There is no help text under any row, and no indenting: a sub-setting is sequenced by position and by appearing at all.

Storage keys keep their `vocab-` / `srs-` prefixes and are listed in the SRS settings table below (the same suffixes apply to `vocab-`). **Vocab Drill and Anime Vocab deliberately share the `vocab-` namespace** — they always have; they are the same drill over different words. `initialDrillSettings(prefix)` migrates the old keys on first read (see `useDrillSettings.test.js` for the exact mapping) and the new keys are written from then on; the retired keys are left in place rather than deleted.

### Details panel (`CardDetails`)

The example sentence and the kanji breakdown live under the card, not on its back — the back is only word, reading and meaning. Designed in the Drill Card Context labs (`src/labs/drill-context/`, iterations 1–7); read those before relitigating the layout.

- **No tokenizer in the browser.** A Tanaka sentence's `dictionary_ids` already name its words, so `tokenizeSentence` finds each entry's forms in the text (longest match first; an inflecting word by its stem) and takes readings and glosses from the dictionary. Unmatched text stays plain. A curated sentence (Anime Vocab's subtitle line, an imported card's own) has no index, so only the card's word is picked out.
- **Known** (`useKnownWords`): words of every drilled textbook chapter except the current one, plus review cards past New — the same rule in both drills. The Furigana "New" mode drops readings on these; a kanji tile lists them under "You know" beside the lesson's / review's / episode's own words.
- **Dark by default.** An outline on the page (`OUTLINE_PALETTE`), keeping light for the card being tested; the sun in the panel's corner switches to paper (`PAPER_PALETTE`) and writes `detailsTheme`. Chosen in *Labs/Details Panel Dark*, whose other palettes go through the `palette` prop.
- **Arriving and leaving:** a drill holds its opening screen back until the first card's word, sentence and kanji meanings are in (`useDrillEntrance` + CardDetails's `onReady`, capped at 1.5s), then everything fades in at once — it used to arrive in three steps. Between cards, `leaving` fades the old words out with the answered card and the new ones fade in with the next. The lookup hooks read their module caches synchronously (`peek*`), so a card that remounts with cached data never renders a blank first frame, and `usePrefetchCardDetails` loads the next cards' sentences and kanji ahead.
- **Timing:** the redaction crossfade is 500ms from the tap, against the card's 450ms flip, both ways. The panel keeps its DOM node across the flip (keyed per card, not per face).
- **Sentence audio** has no recordings yet (an imported card's own `sentenceAudio` aside), so the backup voice reads it — replay button, and Play sentence after the word on a flip.
- **Holding still.** The slot under the card reserves two lines of sentence plus translation and tiles, measured from the rendered panel; a longer sentence steps its type down (to 80%) until it fits (`useFitLines`), so the buttons don't move from card to card. `DrillHUD` drops its reserved streak/hint slot when the streak is off and the caller uses no hint or error — 64px of empty space that pushed the counts below the fold on laptops and phones. The light palette carries a transparent 1px border so switching themes moves nothing.
- **The drill's actions live in a bottom bar** (`DrillActionBar`, chosen after *Labs/Drill Screen Height*): Undo as a square leading the button row (`DrillButtonRow`'s `undo`, `DrillUndoButton`), "Flip card [Space]" before the flip (`DrillFlipButton`, the same height as the verdict buttons), and the session's counts — beside the buttons on desktop, under them on a phone. It's `ActionBar` in its `inFlow` form, in a `position: sticky; bottom: 0` slot at the end of the drill's scroll area (Vocab Drill portals it there from `ActiveDrill`; Reviews renders it in place), so it spans the drill and not the settings sidebar, and the card, panel and credit scroll under it. `DrillHUD` then gets `showUndo={false} showCounts={false}`. With the buttons pinned, the details panel hugs its content (no box stretched around a missing sentence). On desktop its slot reserve stays as invisible room under it, so the vertically centred stage — and its card — never moves between cards. A phone pins the stage to the top instead (card straight under the header) and passes CardDetails `reserve={false}`: the card holds still without the invisible room, which on a short phone was most of what made the page scroll. The bar is in the flow, so the ordinary flex footer layout holds: the stage takes the height left over, the compact credit (`AttributionFooter compact`, "Show more" / "Show less") sits at the foot of the main area just above the bar, and the page only scrolls when the stage genuinely needs it. The credit belongs at the foot of the page (EDRDG's licence); the settings sidebar alone would not do (it can be closed, and it's hidden on phones). Anime Vocab keeps the same row in flow under its card (SpeedModeControls `onFlip`/`onUndo`) — its page layout isn't a fixed-height drill area. A shrinking card was tried and rejected.
- **Word lookup** is the shared `WordPopup` (Definition Popover), passed the drill's own `vocab-srs` progress so "Add to review deck" can't be overwritten by the drill's next save.
- **Sizing:** the card and panel size in `cqw` against the drill's scroll area (`containerType: 'inline-size'`), so an open settings sidebar can't push them wider than the room beside it. Desktop: 380px card, 620px panel, both 6px corners (`.fc-rounded`). Phone: both full width, square.
